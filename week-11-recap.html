The Western 20: Week 11 Recap

UNLV knocks New Mexico from the Mountain West lead, Wyoming takes over the top spot, Boise State outlasts Oregon State under the GameDay lights, and Washington State hangs 73 on Colorado State.

By Tyler Angerbauer | Nov 16, 2026 | Eleven games across the West

Conference races are being shuffled, and teams many thought were done for the season picked up big wins. Chaos reigns supreme in the Western 20.

From Mount Pleasant, Michigan to Honolulu, Hawaii, let’s examine what happened to the Western 20 across the country last week.

Sacramento State vs. Central Michigan

Final | Sacramento State 7, Central Michigan 28

A 37-yard Isaiah Ene touchdown run in the mid second quarter tied Sacramento State’s game against Central Michigan at seven apiece, but the Chippewas would score 21 unanswered to hand the Hornets their ninth loss of a forgettable first season at the FBS level.

Brian Salazar, Jr. started in place of an injured Jackson Sharman for Sacramento State at quarterback, but could only muster 103 yards and an interception. After the touchdown drive, Salazar, Jr. could only lead the Hornets’ offense to just 73 total yards in the remaining 2+ quarters.

Central Michigan dominated the scene on the ground against the Hornets, totaling 319 yards and all four touchdowns. Nick Wolford and Jayden Clerveaux led the way for the Chippewas, combining for 219 yards and a touchdown. The 319 rushing yards allowed are the most in a game this season for the Hornets.

Sacramento State falls to 2-9 overall and 1-7 in their final game of MAC play. They go on bye next week before traveling to Hawaii to close out their season on November 28.

New Mexico State vs. Missouri State

Final | New Mexico State 21, Missouri State 27 (OT)

New Mexico State has not had a good season, averaging just 17 points a game in CUSA play, second worst in the conference, but an overtime loss on the road against Missouri State is a good step in the right direction for Tony Sanchez’s squad.

James Jones had another great day for the Aggies, running for 150 yards and a touchdown to cross 1,000 rushing yards on the year. His 1,090 yards are 11th best in the country. Backup TJ Washington also had a great day, adding 107 yards on just 15 carries for New Mexico State.

Trey Hedden made his second start at quarterback after missing a month, and had a so-so performance, throwing for just 110 yards, two touchdowns, and two interceptions.

The Aggies took the lead mid-way through the fourth quarter on Jones’ one-yard touchdown plunge, but a Connor Lair five-yard touchdown run with 56 seconds to go tied the game up for the Bears. An 88-yard pick-6 in the first overtime period sealed Missouri State’s comeback and gave New Mexico State its third straight loss.

The Aggies fall to 3-7 alongside a 2-4 conference record. They return home to take on Delaware Saturday afternoon.

Nevada vs. Northern Illinois

Final | Nevada 28, Northern Illinois 7

Nevada needed a get-right game after two-straight losses where they were outscored 58-3, and facing a winless Northern Illinois squad was just what the Wolf Pack needed.

Wolf Pack running back Herschel Turner had his best game in over a month, rushing for 153 yards and two touchdowns. Turner had been limited to just 94 yards on the ground over the past four games. This marks the third time Turner has surpassed the century mark on the ground this season.

Northern Illinois was held to one score or less for the third time this season. A DeAree Rogers five-yard score with 4:16 to go in the fourth quarter helped the Huskies avoid their second shutout loss of the year.

At 0-10, the Huskies are on track for their worst season in school history, surpassing the 1997 squad that went 0-11. They remain the only team at the FBS level without a win this season.

Nevada moves to 4-6 on the year and 3-3 record in Mountain West play. Northern Illinois falls to 0-10 with an 0-6 record in their first year as Mountain West members.

Next up, the Wolf Pack returns to Reno to take on Hawaii while Northern Illinois travels to Fargo to face fellow Mountain West newcomer North Dakota State.

Fresno State vs. Texas State

Final | Fresno State 35, Texas State 37

Despite facing a 10-point deficit late in the third quarter, the Fresno State Bulldogs mounted two late touchdown drives to take a four point lead over the surprisingly-potent offense of Texas State. However, a Davian Jackson touchdown run with one minute left helped seal the comeback for the Bobcats, despite entering the game as 11.5 point favorites.

Texas State now stands as the second best offense in the country, based on yards per game, after their 550 yard performance against the Bulldogs. Brad Jackson had his second highest passing yard total in a game following his 355 yard, three touchdown performance. His 2,502 passing yards are 15th best in the country.

Three Bobcat receivers were the main beneficiary of Jackson’s excellent performance, with Beau Sparks, Chris Dawn, Jr., and Zechariah Sample all recording at least 95 receiving yards. On the ground, Torrance Burgess, Jr. continued his excellent season on the ground with 107 yards, his sixth game with 100 or more rushing yards. With 1,225 rushing yards, he stands with the fourth most in the nation.

Fresno State got a good game from Jayden Mandal, who threw for 250 yards, a touchdown, and an interception. Nine different receivers caught a pass from Mandal, with Josiah Freeman leading the way with six receptions for 69 yards.

Despite the paltry offensive numbers for the Bulldogs, 310 for Fresno State versus 550 for Texas State, two special teams touchdowns made the game much closer than the Bobcats were expecting. A 100-yard kickoff return touchdown on the Bulldogs’ first offensive possession and a 19-yard fake field goal late in the third quarter helped keep Fresno State in the game.

With the loss, Fresno State drops to 5-5 and a 2-4 conference record. Texas State jumps to 6-4 on the year with a 4-2 Pac-12 record, tied for second alongside San Diego State.

Fresno State returns home after a two-game roadtrip to take on hapless Colorado State while Texas State faces Washington State in their final regular season home game.

Oregon State vs. Boise State

Final | Oregon State 41, Boise State 46

In the Elevation Change Game of the Week, CFP No. 24 Boise State outlasted Oregon State 46-41 in a game that saw a combined 1,026 yards between the two teams.

Boise was hosting ESPN’s College GameDay for the second time in franchise history, and they were not disappointed by the game and the atmosphere the Broncos provided.

Maddux Madsen returned from a one-game absence and had his best game of the season. His 371 yards and four touchdowns are the most he’s passed for in a game this year. Receiver Rasean Jones, who has been on fire these last three weeks, played the best game of his young career, catching a career-high eleven passes for 230 yards and three touchdowns. The extremely talented freshman has three straight games with 100+ yards and alongside seven touchdowns caught.

Jones’ Beaver counterpart Jesse Legree returned to form after two ‘down’ games. Legree finished with 12 receptions for 140 yards and a touchdown. The 12 receptions were a career high. Legree also padded his league-leading receiving yard total to 1,349 despite playing his first game with Braden Atkinson at quarterback.

Backup Maalik Murphy took over for Oregon State and showcased why he’s one of the premiere backup quarterbacks across college football. Murphy finished with a career high 396 yards and three touchdowns for the Beavers, surpassing his previous career high of 332 yards while playing for Duke in 2024. Outside of Legree, Murphy found great rapport with Adonis McDaniel, who finished with four receptions for 92 yards.

Despite the close final score, the Broncos stayed in control for most of the game. After a 58-yard pick-6 by Boise’s Travis Anderson put Boise up 15-7 in the first quarter, the Broncos led the rest of the game.

With the loss, Oregon State drops to 5-5 on the season with a 3-3 conference record. Boise State jumps to 9-1 with a perfect 6-0 record in their first Pac-12 season.

Oregon State returns home to take on Utah State for their final home game this season while Boise State takes on San Diego State in Boise on Saturday.

San Jose State vs. Air Force

Final | San Jose State 3, Air Force 24

For the third time this season, Air Force won a game without completing a pass in their dominant 24-3 win against San Jose State.

The nation’s best rushing offense shows why they deserve that title, with three Falcons recording 100 or more rushing yards. Quarterback Evan Medders led the flock with 146 yards and a touchdown, the second most he’s had in a game this season. Kemper Hodges added 117 yards and two touchdowns while Owen Allen had 103 yards on the ground.

San Jose State freshman Daniel Rolovich started his third game of the season, but left with an injury early in the second quarter. Benched starter Luke Weaver filled his shoes, but struggled to just 138 yards and no touchdowns in his 2+ quarters of work.

The Spartans crossed the 50-yard line just four times, and their only two scoring opportunities came from two field goal attempts in the third quarter, with kicker Trajan Sinatra only making one of them.

San Jose State falls to 5-6 and a 3-4 Mountain West record. Air Force improves to 8-2 with a 4-2 conference record.

The Spartans return home to take on UNLV while Air Force remains in Colorado to take on UTEP.

UNLV vs. New Mexico

Final | UNLV 21, New Mexico 17

In perhaps the most surprising game of the week, New Mexico lost their perfect conference record, and any hope they had of a potential CFB Playoff berth, at the hands of the upset-minded UNLV Rebels.

New Mexico had been playing with fire recently, having closer-than-comfortable wins against San Jose State, North Dakota State, and Hawaii within the past month, but two fourth-quarter touchdown drives by UNLV sealed their first conference loss of the year.

After being stifled by Wyoming last week, UNLV quarterback Jackson Arnold led the team in passing and rushing, combining for three total touchdowns in the upset. His 44-yard touchdown run with 7:46 left in the game was the game sealing play that allowed the Rebels’ defense to take over. New Mexico had two drives after Arnold’s touchdown run, but a failed fourth down conversion and an interception allowed UNLV to get a critical win.

Lobos starting quarterback Toa Fa’avae finished with just 40 yards and a touchdown on 7/14 passing. It’s a season-low for Fa’avae, who averages just 108.1 yards per game.

The win allows UNLV to move to 6-4 on the year alongside a 4-2 conference record. The loss drops New Mexico to 8-2 and from the lead in the conference, now sitting behind Wyoming by half a game.

UNLV travels to San Jose State on Saturday while New Mexico has a critical road trip to Wyoming Friday night.

Utah State vs. San Diego State

Final | Utah State 13, San Diego State 30

After two impressive weeks back as the starting quarterback, Utah State’s McCae Hillstead crashed back to earth in an uncompetitive 30-13 loss against San Diego State that saw the Aztecs’ kicker score more points than the Aggies’ offense.

Utah State opened the game with an impressive 11 play, 75 yard drive that ended in a Hillstead fumble-turned-touchdown after backup running back Nehemiah Washington fell on the ball for the score. The Aggies would proceed to be held off of the scoreboard for the next 47 minutes, not scoring until there was 1:10 left in the game on a five-yard pass from Hillstead to Anthony Garcia.

The Aztecs were also suffering on the offensive end, but thanks to the heroics of kicker Cooper DiLeva they were able to secure a huge conference victory. DiLeva finished a perfect 3/3 on field goals while also throwing a 20-yard touchdown in a fake field goal trick play.

Aztec running back Javion Kinnard continued his strong conference play, finishing with 113 yards on the ground, his second highest in a game this season. His backup Luck Sutton out-touched Kinnard 21-19 and recorded 96 yards and two touchdowns to help propel the Aztecs to their third victory in their last four games.

Utah State drops back to .500 in conference play, sitting at 3-3 alongside a 4-6 overall record. The Aztecs now stand tied for second, alongside Texas State, with a 4-2 record and a 5-5 overall record.

Next, Utah State plays their final road game of the season at Oregon State while San Diego State faces a massive test with a road trip to CFP No. 24 Boise State.

Wyoming vs. UTEP

Final | Wyoming 27, UTEP 15

In a big twist of events, Wyoming now stands atop the Mountain West standings after having a brutal start to the season. A five game win streak has put the Pokes up top in the conference after New Mexico’s shocking loss against UNLV.

Wyoming had Landon Sims making his first career start due to Tyler Hughes being re-injured in last week’s win against UNLV. Sims had an excellent game, throwing for 289 yards and a touchdown on 19/25 passing. Cowboys’ running back Samuel Harris also had a good game, finishing with a combined 148 yards and two touchdowns.

It was a messy game on the defensive end for both teams. Wyoming lost three fumbles and UTEP lost two, the highest combined fumble total in a game in the Mountain West this season.

UTEP had great production from Raymond Moore III, who finished with just 87 yards through the air but added 164 yards and a touchdown on the ground. The 164 yards are the most in a game this season for Moore III and the second game in which he’s posted more than 100 yards rushing. No other UTEP skill player recorded more than 33 total yards.

Wyoming moves to 7-3 on the year with a near-pristine 6-1 conference record. UTEP falls to 1-9 after their eighth straight loss and remains winless in Mountain West play at 0-6.

Next up, the Pokes return to Laramie in a pivotal matchup against New Mexico on Friday night while UTEP goes on the road to take on Air Force, also on Friday.

Colorado State vs. Washington State

Final | Colorado State 7, Washington State 73

If the UNLV upset over New Mexico wasn’t the most surprising game of the week, then 1-8 Washington State’s absolute obliteration of Colorado State might be.

Washington State set a school record for most points scored against an FBS opponent in a 73-7 drubbing of the worst defense in college football. Nine of the team’s ten touchdowns came on the ground, a school record, and the 535 yards rushing also broke a school record for rushing yards in a game that had stood since 1984.

Four different Cougars scored, with Kirby Vorhees leading the way with four touchdowns and Owen Eshelmen adding three. Vorhees had yet to top 40 yards in a game for over a month and a half, but broke out in a big way with 197 yards on just 12 carries, including a 98-yard breakaway touchdown in the first quarter which is, you guessed it, a school record.

Cougar quarterback Owen Eshelmen, who has been phenomenal since replacing benched Caden Pinnick, finished with a near perfect 13/14 passing alongside 135 yards and a touchdown, in addition to his 81 yards and three rushing scores. The four total touchdowns are the most in a game for the talented dual-threat freshman from Norman, Oklahoma.

Colorado State had a one-yard touchdown plunge by receiver RJ Vick at the end of the second half for their lone score of the night. The 73 points scored is the most the Rams have given up in a game, surpassing the 66 they let up to Texas State earlier in the season. It is the sixth game this nightmare year in which their opponent has scored more than 50 points.

With the loss, the Rams drop to 3-7 overall and fall to the bottom of the Pac-12 with a 1-5 conference record. Washington State, who was winless in conference play up to this point, now stands tied with the Rams in the conference standings while also grabbing just their second victory of the season, their first against an FBS opponent.

Next, Colorado State goes on the road one last time at Fresno State while Washington State travels to San Marcos to take on Texas State.

North Dakota State vs. Hawaii

Final | North Dakota State 21, Hawaii 13

Despite sitting at 8-2 on the year, the North Dakota State Bison have had to fight tougher battles against lower-ranked teams to secure those victories, and that trend continued with a 21-13 comeback victory over Hawaii.

Entering the fourth quarter, the Bison were trailing 3-7 Hawaii by six points, but a touchdown by DJ Scott and a 49-yard pick-6 by Ray James, Jr. on the Rainbow Warriors’ ensuing drive secured the victory for North Dakota State.

Bison quarterback Nathan Hayes had his worst game of the season, throwing for just 97 yards. Coming off a bye, North Dakota State was expecting to come out of the gates stronger, but after a first quarter touchdown, that was practically gifted to them due to a Hawaii interception deep in their own territory, the Bison didn’t enter Rainbow Warrior territory until their fourth quarter touchdown drive. Ultimately, the Bison needed just 183 total yards to secure their fifth conference victory in their first season at the FBS level.

Hawaii had a late touchdown drive in the first half, but otherwise couldn’t do much against one of the nation’s top defenses that recorded five sacks and two interceptions. Kainoa Carvalho was the only Rainbow Warrior who could do much against the Bison, finishing with 63 yards on seven catches.

North Dakota State moves to 8-2 with the win alongside a 5-2 conference record. Hawaii drops to 3-7 and a 2-5 record in Mountain West play with the loss.

Next, the Bison return home to take on Northern Illinois in their final home game this season while Hawaii makes one last road trip on the season to Nevada on Saturday.
