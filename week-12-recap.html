The Western 20: Week 12 Recap

San Diego State stuns Boise State on a last-second field goal, New Mexico retakes the Mountain West lead at Wyoming, Air Force hangs 52 on UTEP, and Fresno State outlasts Colorado State in a shootout.

By Tyler Angerbauer | Nov 22, 2026 | Ten games across the West

Chaos has broken across the Western 20 last week.

Seemingly untouchable Boise State was shockingly defeated at home by San Diego State on a curious end-of-game decision by the Broncos. New Mexico also retook its Mountain West lead and secured its place in the Mountain West championship following a strong victory at Wyoming. The CFP odds of either Boise or New Mexico have dropped considerably over the past two weeks, which leaves the Western 20 in a very precarious situation.

Let’s see what else happened across the west this week.

UTEP vs. Air Force

Final | UTEP 3, Air Force 52

UTEP has one of the worst rushing defenses in the country. Air Force has the best rushing offense in the country. An unstoppable force met a movable object and the Falcons walloped the Miners 52-3 in Colorado Springs on Saturday.

No Air Force player had more than 46 yards rushing, but 20 different Falcons rushed for a combined 429 yards and four touchdowns. Air Force also got involved in the passing game, with Evan Medders and Maguire Martin combining for 157 yards and three touchdowns on 4/5 passing.

The Air Force defense was equally as dominant, holding the Miners to just 116 total yards. Defensive lineman Connor Vasek recorded three sacks, his first of the year, and three TFLs against the Miners. The Falcons defense also won the turnover battle 2-0.

UTEP had a game to forget in an otherwise forgettable season. Raymond Moore III failed to pass 110 yards through the air for the fourth straight game, finishing with just 89 yards and an interception. The 116 total yards by the offense is the lowest they’ve produced all season. Only the 52-17 loss at Michigan comes close, where they finished with just 150 yards.

The Miners fall to 1-10 while still remaining winless in conference play at 0-7. Air Force improves to 9-2 with a 5-2 conference record. Due to tiebreakers, they are unable to make it to the Mountain West championship in two weeks.

Coming up next, UTEP finishes their season in the ‘Pillow Fight of the Week’ at winless Northern Illinois while Air Force has a tough test at New Mexico to close out their regular season.

New Mexico vs. Wyoming

Final | New Mexico 24, Wyoming 17

Coming off a disappointing loss at home against UNLV, the New Mexico Lobos needed a huge win at Wyoming in order to keep their slim CFP chances alive. They opened with a 17-7 halftime lead and held on despite a late Cowboy comeback to win 24-17.

The best defense in the country in yards allowed per game limited Wyoming to just 188 yards while also recording three sacks and eight TFLs. Defensive tackle Xavier Slayton led the way, recording 1.5 sacks, matching his 2025 total, and 2.5 TFLs.

The Lobos offense also found some life with Luke Moga making his first start at quarterback in place of benched Toa Fa’avae. Moga finished with 234 yards and a touchdown through the air while adding 15 yards and a touchdown on the ground.

New Mexico finished with 199 total rushing yards from nine different players. Kiefer Sibley took on the lead back role, after Scottre Humphrey suffered a lower leg injury on the first drive of the game, and finished with 79 yards and a touchdown.

Wyoming had Landon Sims starting his second straight game in place of injured Tyler Hughes. Sims didn’t perform terribly, but it wasn’t enough for a Cowboys offense that couldn’t find its footing outside the fourth quarter.

Facing a 24-7 deficit late in the game, Sims led two straight scoring drives sandwiched around a fumble and a missed field goal by the Lobos. Ultimately, it was too little, too late as the Cowboys failed to secure their spot in the Mountain West Championship.

New Mexico improves to 9-2 on the year with a 6-1 conference record. Wyoming loses their five-game win streak and drops to 7-4 with a 6-2 conference record.

New Mexico finishes the regular season at home against Air Force while Wyoming faces UConn hoping that a North Dakota State loss allows them to sneak into the conference championship.

Delaware vs. New Mexico State

Final | Delaware 42, New Mexico State 30

New Mexico State, despite sitting at 3-8 on the year, showcased its best offensive performance in over a month, but was unable to take the victory against visiting Delaware.

Three Aggies finished with 70 or more receiving yards, with AJ Williams III’s five receptions for 87 yards leading the team. Williams III has broken out lately as the number-one option for Trey Hedden, with 16 receptions for 275 yards and two touchdowns over the course of his last three games.

CUSA rushing leader James Jones also added to his impressive season, finishing with 94 yards on just 11 carries. Jones’ 1,184 yards on the year is 14th best across the country.

The Aggies kept the game close, only trailing by two going into halftime, but 21 unanswered points on the Blue Hens’ first three drives of the second half ended any chance the Aggies had to potentially secure an upset victory.

With the loss, the Aggies fall to 3-8 with a 2-5 CUSA record. Only the 1-10 Sam Houston Bearkats stand between them and the worst spot in the conference.

New Mexico State finishes their season at Middle Tennessee State on Saturday.

Northern Illinois vs. North Dakota State

Final | Northern Illinois 25, North Dakota State 31

After their first drive of the second half, the North Dakota State Bison led the winless Northern Illinois Huskies 31-3 and were looking towards a breezy victory. The Huskies thought differently. Three touchdown drives late in the game brought the score to within six with 2:46 to go, but the Bison were able to hold on in a tight and uncomfortable victory.

Nathan Hayes passed for four touchdowns to four different receivers, a season high, for the Bison. Jackson Williams was his main beneficiary, finishing with 104 yards and a touchdown on six receptions.

The run game was also strong for the Bison, with seven rushers combining for 134 yards on the ground. DJ Scott led the way with 86 yards on just 11 carries.

The Huskies, despite their winless record, had been playing opponents closer over the past month than they had been all season. Taron Dickens led the way in the near comeback, having his best game of the season and finishing with 260 yards and three touchdowns. Receiver DeAree Rogers had one of his best games on the year, with 116 yards and a touchdown on six receptions, while running back Elijah Porter broke the century mark on the ground for the second time in three games, finishing with 107 yards.

Only one Husky drive went for more than 50 yards prior to the three scoring drives in the fourth quarter. But drives of 65 yards, 86 yards, and 96 yards allowed the Huskies to make it a one possession game and force the Bison into a situation they were not prepared for.

The Huskies drop to 0-11 and an 0-7 Mountain West record in their first season in the conference. North Dakota State jumps to a 9-2 record with an impressive 6-2 conference record in its first season at the FBS level.

Northern Illinois finishes the year with one last shot for a win against equally-bad UTEP while North Dakota State faces a win-and-in situation with the Mountain West championship game when they travel to San Jose State on Friday.

Washington State vs. Texas State

Final | Washington State 14, Texas State 42

Washington State was coming off an absolute drubbing of Colorado State where they outscored the Rams 73-7 last week, but crashed back to earth in their 42-14 blowout against Texas State on Saturday night.

The Cougars opened the scoring with a Daniel Blood 22-yard touchdown reception, but the Bobcats scored 42 unanswered points before a 30-yard fumble return touchdown with 5:18 to go gave the Cougars one more score.

Cougar quarterback Owen Eshelmen had his worst game in his young career, finishing with just 150 yards and a touchdown on 21/33 passing. After finishing with 197 yards on 12 carries and four touchdowns against Colorado State, running back Kirby Vorhees could not repeat with an encore, finishing with just 55 on 12 carries and no touchdowns.

The Bobcats, meanwhile, didn’t put up their normal offensive output, finishing with just 422 yards compared to their season average of 511.3, but still walked away with an easy victory. Torrance Burgess, Jr. and Brad Jackson were the stars of the show, finishing with 146 yards and 127 yards on the ground respectively. Burgess and Jackson scored four of the five Bobcat rushing touchdowns on the day, with Burgess having one and Jackson having three.

The Texas State defense has given up a ton of yards this season, but they were able to hold Washington State to just 255 while also stopping three fourth down conversion attempts. In a season where nearly every game has buckets of points scored, it was a good step forward for the Bobcats.

With the loss, Washington State falls to 2-9 on the year with a dreadful 1-6 conference record. Texas State improves to 7-4 and remains tied for second in the conference with a 5-2 record.

In the first ever conference flex schedule, Washington State was paired up with San Diego State in Pullman while Texas State travels to Logan to take on Utah State hoping for a Washington State victory to make it to the Pac-12 Championship against Boise State.

UNLV vs. San Jose State

Final | UNLV 24, San Jose State 22

Coming off their biggest win of the season, UNLV nearly blew an 11 point lead in their narrow 24-22 victory against San Jose State.

Jackson Arnold, after several mediocre performances, had a solid overall game with 263 total yards and two touchdowns for the Rebels. It’s the fifth game this season, and the fourth in his last five, in which he’s recorded at least 197 yards passing and 60 yards rushing.

San Jose State saw Luke Weaver return to take the starting quarterback position, and he had a solid game with 250 yards and two touchdowns, including a 30-yard touchdown pass with 45 seconds remaining to bring the game within two. Unfortunately, his two-point pass conversion fell incomplete, allowing the Rebels to escape with a narrow victory.

Receiver Carson Clark had one of his best games of the season, finishing with 102 yards and a touchdown. It’s the second time this season he’s topped the century mark, and the first since the Spartans’ victory at UTEP a little over a month ago.

The victory allows UNLV to move to 5-2 in conference while also sporting a 7-4 overall record. San Jose State drops to 3-5 in Mountain West play while also remaining one win away from bowl eligibility with a 5-7 record.

UNLV will finish their regular season at home against Nevada while San Jose State will fight for the ability to play more this season in a home matchup against North Dakota State on Friday.

Colorado State vs. Fresno State

Final | Colorado State 42, Fresno State 49

In a game where defense was seemingly optional, Fresno State secured bowl eligibility for the sixth straight season following a wild 49-42 victory over Colorado State.

Fresno State quarterback Jayden Mandal had an impressive performance, throwing three touchdowns to help lead the Bulldogs to 475 total yards of offense. Running backs Bryson Donelson and Rayshon Luke were also heavily involved, with Donelson breaking free for a 61-yard touchdown run early in the third quarter and Luke contributing three total touchdowns.

The Bulldogs also got an unexpected contribution from their defense, with Simeon Harris returning an interception 35 yards for a touchdown on Colorado State’s opening drive. It was one of the few defensive highlights in a game that saw the two teams combine for over 1,000 yards of offense.

Colorado State quarterback Hauss Hejny had a strong performance despite the loss, leading the Rams to 571 total yards of offense. The Rams trailed 49-27 early in the fourth quarter, but two late touchdown passes from Hejny brought the game within seven points with just 26 seconds remaining.

The Rams even recovered the ensuing onside kick, giving themselves one final opportunity to complete an unlikely comeback, but ran out of time before they could find the end zone.

Fresno State improves to 6-5 on the year with a 3-4 conference record, officially securing bowl eligibility after missing out on a winning regular season record in 2025. Colorado State falls to 3-8 with a 1-6 Pac-12 record, continuing a disappointing first season in the conference.

Coming up next, Fresno State hosts Oregon State in the Pac-12’s flex scheduling finale while Colorado State looks to finish its season on as much of a positive note as they can when they host Boise State.

San Diego State vs. Boise State

Final | San Diego State 22, Boise State 20

Boise State entered Saturday’s matchup against San Diego State with a seemingly clear path to the College Football Playoff, but one questionable fourth-down decision may have ended those hopes. The Aztecs scored nine points in the final two minutes, stunning the No. 15 Broncos 22-20 on a game-winning field goal as time expired.

Trailing 20-13 late in the fourth quarter, San Diego State quarterback Stone Saunders found Jaylon Hawkins for a spectacular 37-yard touchdown pass with 1:51 remaining. Hawkins wrestled the ball away from Boise State defender Sherrod Smith in the end zone, bringing the Aztecs within one point. A failed two-point conversion left San Diego State trailing 20-19.

The Broncos recovered the ensuing onside kick and appeared to have escaped with a narrow victory, but a failed fourth-down conversion gave the Aztecs one final opportunity. Rather than punt on fourth-and-three from the San Diego State 45-yard line, Boise State elected to keep its offense on the field. Quarterback Maddux Madsen was stopped for a one-yard loss, giving the Aztecs excellent field position with 1:31 remaining.

Saunders needed just two completions to move San Diego State into field goal range, allowing Cooper DiLeva to connect on a 42-yard field goal as time expired and secure the biggest upset of the week across the Western 20.

Madsen finished with 311 passing yards and a touchdown for the Broncos, but also threw two interceptions in one of his more disappointing performances of the season. Saunders finished with 227 passing yards and 41 rushing yards for the Aztecs, leading San Diego State to its sixth win of the year.

The loss drops Boise State to 9-2 on the season with a 6-1 conference record, putting a massive dent in its chances of earning the Group of Six’s automatic CFP bid. San Diego State improves to 6-5 with a 5-2 Pac-12 record, securing bowl eligibility and a spot in the conference championship.

With wins next week, the two teams will meet again in Boise on December 4 for the Pac-12 Championship, but first San Diego State travels to Washington State for its flex game while Boise State finishes its regular season looking to rebuild momentum against Colorado State.

Utah State vs. Oregon State

Final | Utah State 24, Oregon State 31

Oregon State had to overcome a sluggish first half against Utah State, but three second-half rushing touchdowns from Jake Reichle allowed the Beavers to secure a 31-24 comeback victory in Corvallis.

Utah State opened the game with a strong offensive performance, jumping out to a 14-0 lead early in the second quarter. Receiver Anthony Garcia connected with Javen Jacobs for a 25-yard touchdown on a trick play before quarterback McCae Hillstead added a five-yard rushing touchdown to give the Aggies an early advantage.

Oregon State quarterback Maalik Murphy struggled to get the offense going early, throwing an interception on the Beavers’ opening drive. However, a 29-yard touchdown pass to Cynai Thomas in the second quarter helped bring Oregon State within seven points at halftime.

Hillstead had his best passing performance of the season, finishing with 277 yards and a touchdown through the air while adding 25 rushing yards. Javon Robinson led the Aggies with 91 receiving yards on five receptions, while Garcia finished with 75 receiving yards and a touchdown to go alongside his passing score.

The Beavers found their rhythm in the second half, with Reichle recording three rushing touchdowns from four yards, one yard and two yards out. The final touchdown came with 3:41 remaining, giving Oregon State its first lead of the game and ultimately securing the victory.

Despite outgaining Oregon State 393-361 in total yards and holding the ball for over 33 minutes, Utah State couldn’t find an answer to the Beavers’ late offensive surge. The Aggies had a chance to reclaim the lead late in the fourth quarter, but a failed fourth-down conversion and two sacks on their final possession ended any hopes of a comeback.

Oregon State improves to 6-5 on the season with a 4-3 conference record, securing bowl eligibility in its first season under the new Pac-12 structure. Utah State falls to 4-7 with a 3-4 conference record, officially eliminating the Aggies from bowl contention.

Coming up next, Oregon State travels to Fresno State for its flex game while Utah State finishes its regular season at home against Texas State, hoping to spoil the Bobcats’ otherwise impressive season.

Hawaii vs. Nevada

Final | Hawaii 8, Nevada 16

In one of the ugliest offensive performances across the Western 20 this week, Nevada kept its bowl hopes alive with a 16-8 victory against Hawaii. The Wolf Pack held the Rainbow Warriors scoreless through the first three quarters and limited Hawaii to just 182 total yards in a defensive battle in Reno.

Nevada quarterback AJ Bianco struggled to get much going through the air, finishing with just 106 passing yards, but contributed 55 rushing yards and the only offensive touchdown for the Wolf Pack. Running back Herschel Turner Jr. also helped lead the Nevada rushing attack, breaking free for a 48-yard run that set up Bianco’s five-yard touchdown early in the third quarter.

Nevada’s defense was the biggest story of the night, holding Hawaii to just 41 rushing yards and 141 passing yards, both part of a season-low 182 total yards for the Rainbow Warriors. Defensive lineman Dylan LaBarbera led the way with a sack and two tackles for loss, helping the Wolf Pack shut down an already struggling Hawaii offense.

The Rainbow Warriors’ offensive struggles were made worse when starting quarterback Micah Alejado left the game with an injury on the opening drive of the third quarter. Backup Bjorn Jurgensen struggled to generate consistent offensive production, and Hawaii failed to capitalize on a muffed Nevada punt that gave the Rainbow Warriors a first-and-goal opportunity at the one-yard line.

Nevada kicker Jacob Fernandez was arguably the most important player of the game, connecting on all three of his field goal attempts from 24, 45 and 38 yards. With neither offense able to consistently move the football, his nine points proved to be the difference in the low-scoring affair.

Hawaii finally found the end zone with 1:38 remaining when Jurgensen connected with Titan Lacaden for a 14-yard touchdown. A successful two-point conversion brought the Rainbow Warriors within eight, but Nevada recovered the ensuing onside kick to secure the victory.

The win improves Nevada to 5-6 on the season with a 4-3 Mountain West record, putting the Wolf Pack just one victory away from bowl eligibility for the first time since 2021. Hawaii drops to 3-8 with a 2-6 conference record, continuing a disappointing season for the Rainbow Warriors.

Nevada finishes its regular season in a massive rivalry matchup against UNLV for the Fremont Cannon, with bowl eligibility on the line. Hawaii returns home to face Sacramento State, hoping to finish an otherwise forgettable season with a victory.
