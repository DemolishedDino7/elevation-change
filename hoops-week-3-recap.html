Men’s Basketball | Week 3 Recap

The Western 23: Week 3 Basketball Recap

Gonzaga falls by one to LSU, Utah State tops 118 points twice, Grand Canyon and Texas State stay perfect, and Air Force drops 107 on Loyola Chicago.

By Tyler Angerbauer | Nov 16, 2026 | 23 teams, six conferences

Week 3 brought the first significant setbacks for several preseason contenders. Gonzaga suffered a heartbreaking one-point loss to LSU, New Mexico dropped its first game at Santa Clara, and Boise State’s explosive offense ran into a wall against Nebraska. Meanwhile, Utah State continued its remarkable scoring pace, Grand Canyon remained unbeaten, and Air Force recorded an encouraging win over Loyola Chicago.

Let’s go across the country and see the biggest stories from last week’s action.

Pac-12

Gonzaga

Season 2–1 | This week 0–1

Best game
81–80 loss vs. LSU
Games and results
  • Nov. 14: L 80–81 vs. LSU

Gonzaga suffered its first defeat of the season in heartbreaking fashion, falling 81–80 to LSU in Spokane. After opening the campaign with an impressive victory over Purdue and a dominant performance against New Mexico State, the Bulldogs faced another significant test against SEC competition. The one-point defeat demonstrated how thin the margin for error can be against quality opponents, even for a program with Gonzaga’s offensive capabilities. Mark Few’s team has now been involved in two close contests against major-conference opponents, splitting the pair. While the loss is disappointing, the Bulldogs have already shown they can compete with nationally relevant competition and remain one of the Pac-12’s leading contenders.

Utah State

Season 4–0 | This week 2–0

Best game
120–82 win vs. UC San Diego
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: W 118–86 vs. Utah Tech
  • Nov. 13: W 120–82 vs. UC San Diego

Utah State continued its extraordinary offensive start to the season, scoring at least 118 points in consecutive victories over Utah Tech and UC San Diego. The Aggies opened their week with a 118–86 win over the Trailblazers, highlighted by a 35-point performance from Dallin Hall. Three days later, Mason Falslev took center stage with 33 points and 12 rebounds in a dominant 120–82 victory against UC San Diego. Through four games, the Aggies are averaging an astonishing 105.5 points per contest, establishing themselves as one of the nation’s most explosive offenses. The Ben Jacobson era has begun with four consecutive double-digit victories, and Utah State appears increasingly capable of challenging Gonzaga for Pac-12 supremacy.

Oregon State

Season 4–0 | This week 2–0

Best game
94–84 win vs. Eastern Washington
Games and results
  • Nov. 9: W 94–84 vs. Eastern Washington
  • Nov. 13: W 81–65 vs. Mississippi Valley State

Oregon State maintained its perfect start under first-year head coach Justin Joyner, improving to 4–0 with victories over Eastern Washington and Mississippi Valley State. The Beavers faced their closest test of the season against Eastern Washington, surrendering 84 points but generating enough offensive production to secure a 94–84 victory. They followed that performance with a more controlled 81–65 win against Mississippi Valley State, showcasing an improved defensive effort. Oregon State has now scored at least 81 points in each of its four games, demonstrating the offensive consistency that has become an early trademark of Joyner’s team. Although the Beavers have yet to face a major-conference opponent, their undefeated start provides momentum heading into more demanding competition.

Boise State

Season 3–1 | This week 1–1

Best game
111–79 win vs. Oakland
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 111–79 vs. Oakland
  • Nov. 15: L 75–102 vs. Nebraska (Sioux Falls, South Dakota)

Boise State’s week featured two dramatically different performances, as the Broncos followed another explosive offensive showing with their first loss of the season. Leon Rice’s team opened the week by dismantling Oakland 111–79, marking its third consecutive game with at least 100 points. However, the Broncos encountered a considerably more difficult challenge against Nebraska at the Sanford Pentagon, falling 102–75 to the Cornhuskers. The 27-point defeat represented a significant departure from Boise State’s early dominance and raised questions about its ability to sustain its offensive efficiency against stronger competition. The Broncos remain a dangerous team offensively, but their performance against Nebraska provides an important reminder of the challenges awaiting them in the rebuilt Pac-12.

Colorado State

Season 3–1 | This week 1–1

Best game
87–71 win vs. Portland State
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 87–71 vs. Portland State
  • Nov. 14: L 58–73 vs. Loyola Chicago

Colorado State experienced its first setback of the season after opening the week with a convincing victory over Portland State. The Rams defeated the Vikings 87–71 behind a 21-point performance from Josh Pascarelli, improving to 3–0 in their inaugural Pac-12 campaign. However, their offensive momentum disappeared against Loyola Chicago, as the Ramblers held Colorado State to just 58 points in a 73–58 defeat. The loss marked a significant contrast from the Rams’ previous performances, during which they had scored at least 78 points in every contest. Ali Farokhmanesh’s team has demonstrated encouraging offensive potential, but the difficult outing against Loyola Chicago highlights the importance of developing consistency against stronger defensive opponents.

Texas State

Season 5–0 | This week 3–0

Best game
85–65 win vs. Southern Miss
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: W 72–65 at UTRGV
  • Nov. 13: W 85–65 vs. Southern Miss
  • Nov. 15: W 87–67 at UTSA

Texas State continued its undefeated start with consecutive victories over UTRGV and Southern Miss, improving to 5–0 in its first season as a Pac-12 member. The Bobcats faced a competitive road test against UTRGV, earning a 72–65 victory behind 26 points from Dimp Pernell. Texas State followed that performance with a convincing 85–65 win over former Sun Belt rival Southern Miss, demonstrating a more balanced offensive attack. The Bobcats have now won their first five games by an average of 27 points, although that figure is heavily influenced by their dominant victory over Howard Payne. Texas State’s unbeaten start is encouraging for a program navigating a major conference transition, but more challenging opponents will provide a better indication of its postseason potential.

San Diego State

Season 4–0 | This week 2–0

Best game
73–61 win vs. New Mexico State
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 88–75 vs. Bethune-Cookman
  • Nov. 15: W 73–61 vs. New Mexico State

San Diego State preserved its undefeated start with victories over Bethune-Cookman and New Mexico State, continuing to build momentum in its first Pac-12 campaign. The Aztecs opened the week with an 88–75 win over Bethune-Cookman, showcasing an efficient offense that has been one of the program’s early strengths. They followed that performance with a 73–61 victory over New Mexico State at the Compete 4 Cause Classic, demonstrating an ability to win in a more defensive-minded contest. Brian Dutcher’s team has now won four consecutive games, including an earlier victory over Colorado, and appears capable of competing near the top of its new conference. San Diego State’s combination of offensive efficiency and defensive experience makes the Aztecs a dangerous opponent as the nonconference schedule progresses.

Fresno State

Season 3–1 | This week 2–0

Best game
95–92 win vs. Georgia State
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: W 95–92 vs. Georgia State
  • Nov. 14: W 89–80 vs. UC Riverside

Fresno State bounced back from its disappointing loss to UC San Diego with a pair of victories, improving to 3–1 in its first Pac-12 season. The Bulldogs opened their week with a thrilling 95–92 victory over Georgia State, surviving a high-scoring contest that demonstrated both their offensive potential and defensive vulnerabilities. Fresno State followed the win with an 89–80 performance against UC Riverside, giving the Bulldogs consecutive victories for the first time this season. Although Fresno State has struggled to consistently limit opposing offenses, the team’s ability to generate nearly 90 points per contest this week provides an encouraging development. Continued defensive improvement will be essential as the Bulldogs prepare for the more demanding opponents awaiting them in conference play.

Washington State

Season 1–3 | This week 1–1

Best game
72–71 loss at Duquesne
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: L 71–72 at Duquesne
  • Nov. 14: W 123–87 vs. Northwest University

Washington State finally secured its first victory of the season, although the Cougars’ opening week struggles continued with a heartbreaking road defeat. Washington State fell 72–71 at Duquesne, suffering its third consecutive loss to begin the campaign. The Cougars responded with an overwhelming 123–87 victory over Northwest University, securing their first win under the new Pac-12 banner. Sebastian Akins led Washington State with 27 points in the victory, providing an encouraging offensive performance for a team that had struggled to score consistently. While the Cougars’ first win provides some relief, their three opening defeats suggest Washington State still has substantial work to do before becoming competitive against the Pac-12’s upper-tier programs.

Mountain West

New Mexico

Season 3–1 | This week 1–1

Best game
90–85 loss at Santa Clara
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: W 94–69 vs. Little Rock
  • Nov. 14: L 85–90 at Santa Clara

New Mexico suffered its first defeat of the season after beginning the week with another dominant offensive performance. The Lobos defeated Little Rock 94–69 behind an outstanding 39-point performance from Jake Hall, continuing the sophomore guard’s impressive start to the year. However, New Mexico struggled to contain Santa Clara’s perimeter shooting in its first road test, falling 90–85 to the Broncos. Hudson Mayes delivered a career-best performance with 24 points and 12 rebounds, but his efforts were not enough to overcome Santa Clara’s offensive efficiency. The loss raises early questions about New Mexico’s defensive consistency, particularly against opponents capable of generating scoring opportunities from beyond the arc. Despite the setback, Eric Olen’s team remains one of the Mountain West’s leading conference championship contenders.

Grand Canyon

Season 4–0 | This week 2–0

Best game
99–71 win vs. Cal Poly
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 99–71 vs. Cal Poly
  • Nov. 14: W 88–52 vs. UL Monroe

Grand Canyon continued its undefeated start with two dominant victories, further establishing itself as one of the Mountain West’s strongest early-season teams. The Antelopes opened their week with a 99–71 victory over Cal Poly before overwhelming UL Monroe 88–52, limiting the Warhawks to just 52 points. Bryce Drew’s team has now won each of its first four games by at least 18 points, demonstrating impressive consistency on both ends of the floor. Jaden Henley has emerged as one of the Lopes’ most reliable offensive contributors, while their balanced supporting cast has allowed Grand Canyon to generate scoring opportunities throughout the rotation. Although the Antelopes have yet to encounter a major national contender, their dominant opening performances suggest they are ready to challenge New Mexico for the conference championship.

Nevada

Season 2–2 | This week 0–2

Best game
73–71 loss vs. Utah Valley
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: L 63–76 at UC Santa Barbara
  • Nov. 14: L 71–73 vs. Utah Valley

Nevada’s promising start took a difficult turn this week, as the Wolf Pack suffered consecutive defeats following their impressive overtime victory against Saint Mary’s. Steve Alford’s team struggled offensively in a 76–63 road loss to UC Santa Barbara before returning home and falling 73–71 to Utah Valley. The narrow defeat against the Wolverines was particularly frustrating for a program hoping to build momentum in Alford’s final season in Reno. After scoring 96 points against Saint Mary’s, the Wolf Pack failed to surpass 71 points in either of this week’s contests, revealing concerns about their offensive consistency. Nevada remains capable of challenging the Mountain West’s top teams, but the Wolf Pack must rediscover the scoring production that highlighted their season-opening victories.

Wyoming

Season 3–1 | This week 1–1

Best game
76–63 loss at North Carolina
Games and results
  • Nov. 10: L 63–76 at North Carolina
  • Nov. 14: W 76–53 at Denver

Wyoming faced its toughest challenge of the young season with a road matchup against North Carolina, remaining competitive before ultimately falling 76–63 to the Tar Heels. The Cowboys trailed by just two points at halftime, demonstrating an ability to compete with a major-conference opponent before North Carolina pulled away in the second half. Nasir Meyer led Wyoming with 17 points in the defeat, providing an encouraging performance against quality competition. The Cowboys responded with a convincing 76–53 road win against Denver, improving to 3–1 overall. Sundance Wicks’ team has now demonstrated both its potential against nationally relevant competition and its ability to handle regional opponents. Wyoming’s early performances suggest the Cowboys could become a difficult matchup for teams throughout the Mountain West.

UNLV

Season 3–0 | This week 1–0

Best game
76–68 win vs. Stanford
Games and results
  • Nov. 13: W 76–68 vs. Stanford

UNLV improved to 3–0 with its most impressive victory of the young season, defeating Stanford 76–68 in Las Vegas. After beginning the campaign with victories over Northern Arizona and Samford, the Runnin’ Rebels faced their first major-conference opponent and responded with a convincing performance. The win represents an encouraging step for Josh Pastner’s program, which is attempting to return to the NCAA Tournament for the first time since 2013. UNLV has now scored at least 76 points in each of its opening three contests while demonstrating an ability to win against increasingly difficult competition. The Rebels’ undefeated start gives them momentum heading into the remainder of their nonconference schedule, where additional opportunities await to strengthen their postseason résumé.

Hawai’i

Season 3–1 | This week 0–1

Best game
73–66 loss vs. Portland
Games and results
  • Nov. 9: L 66–73 vs. Portland

Hawaii suffered its first defeat of the season, falling 73–66 to Portland in the finale of the OUTRIGGER Rainbow Classic. The Rainbow Warriors struggled offensively during the second half, missing 18 consecutive field-goal attempts while attempting to overcome the Pilots. Despite the extended scoring drought, Hawaii remained within striking distance but ultimately could not complete the comeback. The loss marked a frustrating conclusion to an otherwise successful opening stretch that included victories over Seattle, Bethesda University and UT Arlington. Eran Ganot’s team has demonstrated encouraging potential, but its offensive struggles against Portland highlight the importance of developing consistent scoring options. As the Rainbow Warriors prepare for their first Mountain West conference schedule, avoiding lengthy offensive droughts will be essential against stronger opponents.

UC Davis

Season 3–1 | This week 2–0

Best game
75–56 win at Sacramento State
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 81–69 vs. Incarnate Word
  • Nov. 14: W 75–56 at Sacramento State

UC Davis continued its recovery from a disappointing season-opening defeat, winning consecutive games against Incarnate Word and Sacramento State to improve to 3–1. The Aggies began with an 81–69 victory over Incarnate Word, highlighted by a 21-point performance from Brayden Fagbemi. UC Davis followed that result with an impressive 75–56 road victory against Sacramento State, holding the Hornets to just 56 points while securing its third consecutive win. Connor Sevilla led the Aggies with 16 points against Sacramento State, while Jalen Stokes contributed 10 rebounds. The victory provides an encouraging sign for a team entering its first Mountain West campaign, particularly given its ability to control a road matchup against another program navigating conference realignment. UC Davis has demonstrated steady improvement after its opening loss and appears increasingly comfortable with its new roster.

Air Force

Season 3–2 | This week 1–1

Best game
107–93 win vs. Loyola Chicago
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 107–93 vs. Loyola Chicago
  • Nov. 15: L 69–93 at Pacific

Air Force delivered one of the week’s more surprising offensive performances, defeating Loyola Chicago 107–93 at Clune Arena. The Falcons were led by Lucas Hobin’s 26 points, while Kam Sanders contributed 12 assists in an impressive display of offensive execution. The victory marked Air Force’s third consecutive win, providing additional evidence of the progress made under first-year head coach Joe Crispin. However, the Falcons could not carry that momentum into their rescheduled road contest against Pacific, falling 93–69 after struggling through an inefficient first half. Air Force’s contrasting performances highlight both the promise and inconsistency of its new offensive system. Although the program has already surpassed last season’s three-win total in competitive Division I matchups, establishing a reliable defensive identity remains a major challenge.

UTEP

Season 2–1 | This week 1–0

Best game
60–58 win vs. New Mexico State
Games and results
  • Nov. 12: W 60–58 vs. New Mexico State

UTEP earned an important victory in its renewed rivalry with New Mexico State, defeating the Aggies 60–58 in a tightly contested matchup. The Miners entered the game seeking to build on their earlier win against Incarnate Word and responded with a defensive-minded performance that secured their second consecutive victory. Cam McDaniels led UTEP with 22 points, providing nearly 37 percent of the team’s offensive production in the narrow win. Although the Miners struggled to generate consistent scoring throughout the contest, their ability to secure a close victory against a familiar rival represents an encouraging development for Joe Golding’s rebuilt roster. UTEP has now improved to 2–1, and its defensive performance against New Mexico State offers a foundation for the program as it prepares for Mountain West competition.

Summit League

North Dakota State

Season 2–4 | This week 1–1

Best game
82–81 overtime win vs. Illinois State
Games and results
  • Nov. 13: W 82–81 vs. Illinois State (OT)
  • Nov. 15: L 69–73 vs. Cal State Bakersfield

North Dakota State experienced another challenging week, splitting two closely contested games as the Bison continue adjusting to their substantially rebuilt roster. The defending Summit League champions earned a thrilling 82–81 overtime victory over Illinois State, with Max Iversen leading the way with 27 points. However, the Bison were unable to build on that momentum, falling 73–69 to Cal State Bakersfield two days later. North Dakota State struggled mightily from beyond the arc against the Roadrunners, converting just three of its 29 three-point attempts while shooting 36 percent overall. Rob Diaz III led the Bison with 16 points and nine rebounds, but the offensive struggles ultimately proved too difficult to overcome. After a 27-win campaign last season, North Dakota State has already suffered four defeats and faces a significant challenge in rebuilding its championship-level consistency.

New Mexico State’s difficult start continued with consecutive defeats against regional opponents, dropping the Aggies to 1–3 overall. Horizon League

Best game
72–64 win at Bellarmine
Games and results
  • Nov. 11: W 82–43 vs. Clarke University
  • Nov. 14: W 72–64 at Bellarmine

Northern Illinois continued its encouraging start under first-year head coach Matt Majkrzak, earning consecutive victories to improve to 3–2 overall. Northern Illinois has already demonstrated improved competitiveness in its inaugural Horizon League campaign, and its recent performances suggest the Huskies may outperform their modest preseason expectations.

Big West

Sacramento State

Season 1–2 | This week 0–1

Best game
75–56 loss vs. UC Davis
Games and results
  • Nov. 14: L 56–75 vs. UC Davis

Sacramento State suffered its second consecutive defeat, falling 75–56 to UC Davis in its first matchup against a former Big West opponent as a conference member. The Hornets struggled offensively throughout the contest, scoring just 56 points after averaging 83.5 points in their opening two games. Marcus Wilson led Sacramento State with 12 points, but the team’s established offensive contributors were unable to generate the production necessary to challenge the Aggies. The defeat raises early concerns about Sacramento State’s ability to compete against the more disciplined defensive teams on its schedule, particularly after a promising offensive showing against Santa Clara the previous week. Mike Bibby’s team possesses intriguing individual talent, but developing offensive consistency and improving its defensive execution will be essential if the Hornets hope to climb the Big West standings.
